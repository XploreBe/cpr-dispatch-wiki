Overview

Community CPR Dispatch — an R&D record

This wiki records what happened while building an emergency-response application as a testbed for AI-assisted software delivery — and what we learned about process, models, and tooling along the way. The application is the thing we can click on. The method is what we are actually studying.

What this is

Community CPR Dispatch connects emergency dispatchers with nearby trained volunteers who can start CPR before an ambulance arrives. We build it as real software, but its purpose here is research: the product is not the goal. The goal is to learn how humans and AI agents collaborate to ship software reliably, to measure where the real efficiencies and costs are, and to mature a delivery harness — the conventions, tooling, and process around the model.

The reset, in brief

The retrospective's central thesis is worth stating once here, because it explains why the operating model looks the way it does. Most friction in the first attempt was not a tooling or model problem — it came from applying the ceremony and parallelism of a large project to one that was still small and immature. The root cause, stated plainly: we tried to build something too elaborate, too early, before thinking through what it required.

The corollary lessons that shaped the restart:

  • Agree the contract before parallelizing. When two parts share a contract (front/back), either one person holds the whole contract, or the contract is agreed first. Parallelizing without it doesn't save time — it moves the cost, multiplied, into integration.
  • Executable conventions mature with the project. You cannot write a rule for code that does not exist yet. Delegating to cheaper models needs conventions encoded as checks; those only exist once there is structure to distil them from. A "not yet," not a "never."
  • Isolate experiments. A co-edited monorepo with shared pieces (skills, CLAUDE.md) contaminates experiments; conclusions get weaker.
  • Everything the model sees is context. A stale backlog, discarded features left lying around, or business-analysis jargon in a technical spec are all noise injected into the harness. Discarding something means removing it from context, not just marking it discarded.
  • Requirements are incomplete and changing — by default. The process should absorb change rather than assume a complete, static specification.

The guiding principle carried into the restart: zero premature complexity — every sprint introduces only the machinery that sprint's work already demands.
